How it works

Five stages, one continuous pass.

Every script moves through the same sequence of dedicated processing units — each one auditable, each one contributing to the final, explained score.

1

Capture & normalize

Scanned images, PDFs, or camera-captured scripts are ingested and normalized into one standard format.

2

Extract text (OCR)

Denoising, contrast enhancement, and handwriting normalization feed a transformer OCR engine.

3

Embed & compare

Student and model answers become high-dimensional vectors; cosine similarity yields a concept-alignment score.

4

Classify & assess

Relevance classification flags filler and keyword-stuffing while a parallel unit scores grammar and fluency.

5

Score & explain

Rubric weights combine every metric into a final score, with generated feedback delivered to the student and teacher.

Technology & innovation

What actually changes the grading, not just the interface.

The core innovation isn't OCR or embeddings alone — it's separating what was said from how well it was said, and running both through a rubric an educator actually controls. Each unit is independently trained, independently auditable, and combined deterministically.

  • Fine-tuned TrOCR encoder–decoder for handwritten and printed academic text, trained on over 1.1 million answer-sheet samples.
  • Sentence-BERT / MPNet embeddings at 768 dimensions, contrastively fine-tuned on 140,000+ paired student and model answers.
  • Three-way relevance classifier — plausible, partially relevant, irrelevant — trained on manually labelled response data.
  • GPU-accelerated inference on A100/RTX-class hardware, keeping grading close to real time at scale.

FAQ

Questions we get early.

How does it grade a paraphrased but correct answer?

Student and model answers are converted into semantic embeddings and compared by concept alignment, not word overlap — so a correctly paraphrased answer scores on par with one that matches the model answer's wording.

Can teachers control how strict the scoring is?

Yes. The rubric-scoring unit reads teacher-defined weightages for concept alignment, relevance, and language quality from memory — so scoring behavior can be tuned per question without touching the underlying system.

What handwriting quality does it need?

The OCR unit is fine-tuned on over a million handwritten and printed academic samples and applies noise removal, contrast enhancement, and stroke normalization before extraction — it's built for real exam handwriting, not clean typed text.

How does it stop students from gaming the score?

The relevance classification unit analyzes semantic deviation to identify filler content and keyword-stuffing in embedding space, rather than relying on surface-level keyword presence, so inserted keywords without real explanation are discounted.

Can results plug into our existing LMS?

Every evaluation can export as PDF, HTML, or machine-readable JSON, so scores and feedback flow into an existing LMS, student portal, or institutional database.
